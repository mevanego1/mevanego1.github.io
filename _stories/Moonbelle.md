“The greenhouse? Of all things, that is what sold you on this place? You don’t know the first thing about gardening.”

Reya furrowed her brows defensively. “I can learn! The space is so beautiful it would be a crime not to try. Besides, I’m going to have a lot of spare time on my hands when we finish with the move.”

To that, I couldn’t argue. When my job offered me a substantial raise to relocate me to the middle of nowhere midwest I thought that it might spell my relationship with Reya. She would be required to drop everything to come with me to a place where she had no connections. She left her job, her friends, her family, everything that she had ever known to drive across the country and be with me.

The idea of her loving me so deeply was romantic and flattering. But deep down it created a burning guilt because I knew that I didn’t deserve it.

So if a place to grow plants and take up a new hobby was what she needed to feel at home, then it was an easy yes for me.

In the span of less than a week we checked out of our hotel and became the proud new owners of 18 Lotus Lane. We began to unpack our humblingly small pile of worldly possessions from the U-Haul into our new home. I appreciated the house for its quiet. And its price.

In a moment of quiet, I caught myself staring at Reya in her new plant room, with her eyes closed enjoying the late August sun rays that shone through the glass. Her skin radiated under the bright lights, giving her an almost angelic glow.

The greenhouse, or sunroom, whatever the hell you wanted to call it, was the only truly noteworthy thing about the entire house. The room was a sizable structure connected to the dining room, with three walls and a ceiling made almost entirely of thick glass paneled between old wooden beams. Even I had to admit that stepping into the room came with an unavoidable sense of serendipity. I chuckled as I walked past the sunroom and heard Reya hum as she unboxed several pots that she had recently purchased.

I was ready to plant my roots.

My work at the power plant began before we were able to fully settle into our new home. I was hoping that I would have more time to figure out furniture and wall paint and new carpeting, but the week that I had allotted for those tasks had dried up much quicker than I could have anticipated. What I hadn’t yet told Reya was that in addition to my pay bump, I was also looking at a significant increase in my workload. Most days I would need to be out of the house by seven, and I likely wouldn’t return until well past six. On my first day at the job, guilt ate at me as I thought of Reya alone in our home for so many hours.

As soon as my shift ended I got into my car and I sped home, thankful to finally be rid of New York traffic for good. Trees and farms passed in a blur as I cruised down the back country roads. I slowed only when I turned onto our street and noticed a car that I did not recognize parked at the end of our driveway.

I got out of my car, still in my work attire, and hurried to the door.

“Reya?” I called.

“In here!” Her voice responded from the sunroom.

I walked through the house cautiously, floorboards creaking beneath my weight. I went through the dining room and found the sunroom door open. Inside, I heard two voices echoing off the chamber of glass.

“Hello?” I said as I poked my head in.

Instinctively, I sized up our guest. She was a woman that was small in stature but not in shape. She had curly hair that was graying and wore round glasses, appearing like a grandmother from straight out of a cartoon.

“Hello dear.” She replied with a smile.

“Mike, this is Ms. Quinn, she’s our neighbor from down the street. Ms. Quinn, this is Mike, my boyfriend.”

The pair of ladies sat in folding chairs that Reya had moved into the sunroom in lieu of real furniture. They were both holding mugs of what I had assumed to be tea.

“Please, call me Lily. Wonderful to meet you Mike. I have heard such great things. He’s even cuter in person.” Lily said with a wink towards Reya, who laughed into her mug.

“Not sure how much you’ve managed to hear in such a short time.” In my head, it had been a joke. In delivery, it came across as needlessly aggressive.

Reya gave me a scornful look.

“It is a pleasure to meet you, Ms. Quinn.” I said in an attempt to graze past my previous comment.

“Lily.” She corrected.

If Ms. Quinn was offended, she didn’t show it. She stared up at the high glass ceiling of the sunroom. Her eyes traced the old wooden beams holding the structure up.

“The previous owners had done such wonderful things with this room. I only managed to catch glimpses of it in passing. The nosy old lady I am, I’ve always wanted to come inside! Thank you for this wonderful opportunity, Reya.”

Reya’s eyes lit up. “What did they do with the room? I could use all of the advice I can get. I’m new to growing plants, and I think I have the opposite of a green thumb. What would that be, a red thumb? Basically everything that I plant ends up dead.”

Ms. Quinn laughed. “If opinions are what you are after, I know exactly how to help. Once a week, the wives on the block have a little get together. You should come out! The next one is at my house this Friday.”

Ms. Quinn’s face turned to a nasty scowl. “Just don’t listen to a word that Miradeth says. That lying, two-faced bitch.”

Reya laughed loudly as I beat my head gently against the doorframe.

“That sounds perfect.” Reya said, taking Ms. Quinn’s hand. “I am looking forward to it.”

“Well, I do still have a meatloaf in the oven, so I must get going. It was wonderful to meet you both. You have such a marvelous house, thank you again for the tea and the company. It was so nice to meet you both.”

*Wonderful*. The word was beginning to lose meaning.

Ms. Quinn stood to leave, and I walked her to the door. I waved her off and shut the front door, reclaiming my home.

“She seems nice.” Reya called from the kitchen as she turned on the sink faucet.

I walked back to the kitchen and saw Reya washing the two mugs that had been used for tea. She stared down at them, lost in thought.

“Yeah, she’s alright.”

I held my breath for a moment, but eventually I couldn’t bite my tongue any longer.

“You invited a stranger inside? Without me being here? They could have been dangerous.”

Reya cut off the water stream and cocked her head.

“Really Mike? Lily is harmless. She lost her husband a few years ago and she has nothing to do around this barren town. What do you want me to do all day while you are at work? Would you prefer I do housework, or just sit on my thumbs and wait for you to come back?”

I stumbled on my words, failing to form a reply. I had once again been caught being a complete ass.

Reya noticed my struggle and brought down the tension with a cooling tone.

“If this is going to work, you are going to need to give me my space to do my own thing. You know that I can take care of myself. I cannot be apart from you all day,and then be suffocated when you come home. Please, give me the space that I need to grow here.”

“I’m sorry. I will try to be better. I only worry because I care.”

“Good.” Reya took one long step to close the distance between us and planted a kiss on my cheek. “That is all I ask. Because I want you to grow here with me.”

“Do you at least still have the pepper spray that I got you?”

Reya rolled her eyes.

“Yes, it is upstairs in my nightstand.”

“In the nightstand! What good does that do if someone were to attack you down here?”

Reya broke away and began to climb the stairs to the second story. She didn’t bother responding.

“The paint and sheets are out in the guest bedroom, I trust that you can handle adding a few coats before bed? I am deep into a book about gardening, and I am dying to finish it tonight.” She called out as she ascended.

Several moments passed as she continued to creak her way upstairs.

“I’ll take care of it.” I responded in resignation.

“There is the Mike that I know and love!” Reya called, her voice growing more distant as she approached the door to our bedroom.

With a huff of breath, I moved to follow her up the steps, preparing myself for the tedious task of watching paint dry.

“I’m useless! Absolutely useless!” I heard Reya’s voice from deep inside the house after opening the front door.

“What’s the matter?” I called as I hurried towards her. Unsurprisingly, I found her in the greenhouse, with the door wide open. I sighed. I had told her several times that leaving the door open would cause our AC to leak out.

She came at me with hands covered in soil and wrapped herself around my waist. The dirt would find itself at home on my work uniform after a day at the plant.

“Everything I touch dies. Even the snake plant that Cindy swore a toddler could handle shriveled up and withered. I’m helpless.” She said into my chest.

I patted her on the back.

“Uhhh, Did you water them?”

She looked up at me, dumbfoundedly. “Water? Is that something that plants need?”

I cocked my head to the side, my mouth hung agape.

“Of course I watered them! The precise amount on the perfect cadence. I just don’t get what I am doing wrong. There is plenty of light.”

Over the past two weeks we had settled into our routine living at our new home. I would work, admittedly pretty late most days, and then come home and help with dinner. While I was gone, Reya chipped away at the long list of chores that still needed to be done. In her free time, she also went into town often, befriending all of the store owners and locals. Naturally, she was already best buds with Cindy, the owner of Green Garden, the only plant shop within a twenty mile radius.

“Alright, let's take a look. It can’t possibly be that bad.”

Reya hung her head in shame as I entered the greenhouse.

“Oh.” I said as I took in the scene.

Horrid didn’t begin to describe what I was looking at.

The plants weren’t just dead, they had somehow accrued months worth of rot and disease. The pungent smell of decomposition hung in the air and I had to wrinkle my nose. Somehow, even the color of the soil that the plants were potted in had become washed out.

“How?” I asked. “This doesn’t make any sense. There must be parasites in the soil, or some other infestation. This isn’t normal.”

A fruit fly glided past my line of sight. And then I noticed another, and another, until I was able to identify almost two dozen of the annoying little bugs flying around the place.

Another reason to keep the door shut.

“That basil,” Reya pointed. “It came straight from Cindy’s personal herb garden. She said she had the plant for years.”

I exited the sunroom and closed the door behind me. I took a seat at the dining room table.

I navigated my next words carefully.

“Maybe it’s time to consider… new hobbies? Knitting or basket weaving or something. We all have our strengths.”

Reya’s expression turned defiant. “I’m not going to give up.”

“The room will still be a lovely place with a few fake plants. It doesn’t have to be a real garden.”

Reya shook her head. “I can’t do that. I can’t let my first passion in this home end in death. I refuse to cover it up with a facade. It’s silly, Mike, but I feel like if I can give life to something beautiful in that room, then I can do anything.”

I nodded in understanding. It was a personal quest. It wasn’t supposed to mean anything to me. This was Reya’s journey and it was my job to support her in it.

“Ok. I get it. I am in your corner. Anything you need from me, you have it, no questions asked.”

Reya lit up with renewed vigor. She looked absolutely gorgeous when she glowed.

“Actually, I am hosting the neighbors here tomorrow and I have a lot of preparation to do. Would you mind swinging by Green Garden and grabbing some fresh seeds on your way home from work? Maybe all the garden needs is your special touch.”

Reya looked at me with a face that was impossible to say no to. I didn’t mind swinging by the plant shop, but the idea of having all of those guests in our unfinished home did not sit well with me. The place was still a mess, and rather than getting it sorted, Reya would need to focus her energy on hosting and providing. I gritted my teeth.

“Anything I needed, right?”

“Of course, my love.” I said with a forced smile.

I understood how the Green Garden received its name. There was not a single square foot of bare wall in the entirety of the little plant store. All of the walls were covered head to toe in different varieties of flora and fauna.

And they all looked the damned same to me.

“Hey.” I said to a woman  standing behind the front register watering one of the nearby plants. I assumed she must be Cindy. She was young, with blonde hair done in a style that I imagine would have been popular thirty years ago. Around her neck was a gardening apron covered in brown and green stains.

She also had the most severe case of resting bitch face that I had ever seen.

“Welcome to Green Garden. The name’s Cindy. How can I help ya today?”

“I’m Mike, Reya’s boyfriend. You’ve been helping her with tips for the garden.”

“She’s mentioned you.” Cindy said, her expression remaining needlessly contemptuous.

I stared at Cindy, slightly stunned. Reya had mentioned that Cindy had a “heart of gold” and was “one of the kindest people she had ever met”. I had assumed some hyperbole had been involved, but even so, I was struggling to reconcile the woman that stood before me with the person who Reya had described.

“Anyway, I’m just here for a few more seeds. Anything works. Preferably something that won’t die on us this time.” I eyed up the endless walls of vines and stems.

“Us?” Cindy asked, raising an eyebrow. “Thought you’ve been too busy workin’ to help with the gardening.”

Was Reya going to tell our whole life story to everyone in this forsaken town?

I, very maturely, ignored her jab.

“I’ll take this one.”

“That is a calathea. It wouldn’t last five minutes in your sunroom. Incredibly sensitive to sunlight.” Cindy responded without taking her piercing eyes off me.

“Ok, fine. Can you just tell me what you recommend so that I can be on my way?”

“Reya called earlier today. Said that the snake plant and my basil died. That true?”

“Yeah, it’s true.”

Cindy clicked her tongue a few times.

“Alright, I've got something new for you to try. I’ve never actually grown it myself, but it's supposed to be the most resilient flower that you can grow. It’s called a moonbelle. Very pretty, I hear.”

“You expect us to grow something that you have never grown yourself? That is ridiculous. Why can’t I just buy another snake plant? I think the issue was the soil. There must have been pesticides.”

Cindy stared at me for several moments. Eventually, she just walked away. She entered a backroom and then returned several seconds later. She held out her hand, dangling a small inconspicuous package.

“Take these moonbelle seeds and get out of my store. Or, you can buy that snake plant over there for $17.99.”

I huffed as I swiped the packet of seeds from her hand.

“Have a nice day.” I called as the bell on the swinging glass door chimed behind me.

When I returned home, it was already well past seven o’clock. I could tell by the arrangement of cars parked at the foot of our driveway that the gathering of the lotus ladies was still in session.

I wandered inside and was greeted by the noise of laughter and hysterics.

“You dog!” The voice of a woman that I did not recognize shouted. The sounds were coming from the dining room, and I could smell the faint scent of fresh baked goods.

“It’s true! Seven times!” This voice I did recognize as Ms. Quinn.

“What about you, Rey? Mike is quite the looker, I’m sure you two get up to all kinds of freaky shit!”

Several other women hollered in laughter.

I paused for a moment.

What would Reya say about me to a room full of strangers when I wasn’t around to hear?

For some reason, that thought was paralyzing. I didn’t want to eavesdrop, but maybe even more than that I was scared of how she would respond. Like any other couple, our intimacy had its highs and its lows. With the stress of the move and my new hours, I’ll admit that it had dropped pretty low.

“Reya, I got the seeds that you asked for.” I stepped into the dining room and pretended that I wasn’t aware of our guests’ presence. “Oh, hey there.” I said, waving my hand.

Most of the women around our dining room I had never met before. I did however recognize Ms. Quinn and Miradeth, her archnemesis.

My eyes scanned the room as Reya introduced me and rattled off a volley of names that I would soon forget. Most of the ladies looked the same to me. They were all similar in age, at least twenty years older than Reya.

“Sorry, I didn’t mean to interrupt your girl time. I’ll be upstairs reading if you need anything.”

As I turned to leave, Miradeth let out a catcall and Reya began to laugh with all of her new friends. Through the thin walls, I listened to the type of raucous laughter that could only be made when a group of women have had a few too many glasses of wine. By the time the last of the ladies had left I was already in bed with my eyes closed. I wasn’t tired, nor would I be able to fall asleep anytime soon, but Reya didn’t know that as she entered the bedroom. I turned to face her and she smiled, still giddy from her eventful night.

“Thank you for getting the seeds.” She whispered to me. I could tell that she was still a little bit tipsy. “I really think that this one is going to be different.” She planted a kiss on my forehead, and then crashed into the bed without changing her clothes.

“I love you, Mike.” She mumbled in a dazed slumber moments before she began her faint snoring. Moonlight from our porthole window illuminated her peaceful face and I let out a deep, relieving breath.

I smiled and closed my eyes, knowing that sleep would soon come to me as well.

“Look! Look Mike! Look!” I hadn’t been out of my car for more than several seconds before Reya was running down the driveway with a small flowerpot in her hands. “It’s growing! It’s actually growing!”

She didn’t even have shoes on, but she didn’t seem to care as she hoisted the flowerpot into my face. Along the plane of dark brown soil, there were several unimpressive sprouts of green poking through.

“That’s great!” I feigned my enthusiasm. My day at the plant had been particularly challenging, and I was trying desperately to put it behind me. While the little sprout itself did little to move that needle, Reya’s childlike wonder was dangerously contagious.

She did a little dance with the pot in her hand, and I couldn’t help but laugh. Moving to the new house has had its fair share of challenges, but maybe Reya’s stupid little flower meant something. Maybe it was a sign that times ahead would be different.

“Come on.”  I said as I motioned her inside. “Get some shoes on while I shower. This deserves celebrating. Tonight we are eating in town.”

Reya’s grin was ear to ear and she pecked me on the cheek before running back into our house.

She was rambling something to herself about figs or ferns or some other sort of fauna.

To the moonbelle’s credit, it did change everything. The next several weeks were nothing like we had experienced before.

When the sprout finally flowered the petals presented themselves as a mesmerizing gradient of white to light indigo with shades of blue. It was bell-shaped and the flower itself hung low off its stem. I was grateful that the flower had turned out so pretty without ever seeing a picture of the thing. As much as Cindy acted like an ass during our encounter, she did steer us right with the suggestion, and for that, I had to give her props.

Reya had never been so happy. The sunroom became her temple and her sole source of pride. When the moonbelle bloomed it gave Reya the confidence to go back to the Green Garden and reattempt to grow some of the plants that she had struggled with in the past.

It felt like every day that I came home from work another new plant or flower was added to the sunroom. Before long, the place was looking indistinguishable from the walls of Green Garden.

Reya spent every spare minute that she had in that room. Rotating pots, changing soil, watering plants and optimizing their placement for sunlight. It was a jungle that had flourished to life and it had all been at her hand. Through all of the iterations of the sunroom, what had never changed was where she displayed the moonbelle. It sat center stage, displayed proudly and prominently, among the sea of other plants.

Reya had recently hosted the Lotus Lane ladies again and this time, they spent the entire evening in the sunroom. Reya glowed as she was showered in compliments for how nicely she had done in turning the room into a paradise.

Reya’s happiness had overflown from her life and into the lives of everyone around her, including myself. Even my work became more manageable, and after so long, the house started to feel like a home.

But no flower can bloom forever.

One night, Reya brought up a point at dinner that had hardly seemed important at the time.

“We are out of space in the sunroom. I can’t fit another plant in there if I tried.”

I took a bite of my chicken parm, unphased by the notion.

“Fine by me. We really have racked up quite the tab at Green Garden. Maybe it’s time we lay off growing for a bit and appreciate what we have.”

I could tell by Reya’s reaction that I had said the wrong thing.

“If that doesn’t work, we have plenty of space in the backyard to set up garden boxes.” I continued.

Reya shook her head. “It’s nearly the end of autumn. Nothing will grow outside if I plant it now.”

“Well, now that we are settled, you can also consider looking for another teaching gig. I heard there was an opening at a school one town over. We don’t need the extra income from your job, I know that. But maybe you can apply for an environmental science position, or maybe Cindy needs an extra hand at her store. It could be a win-win.”

“Maybe.” She considered, leaving her dinner untouched.

That night, Reya was in bed before me. An uncommon occurrence. I turned off the light and settled in next to her. I tossed and turned for several moments before realizing that I had never kissed her goodnight.

I leaned over to kiss her on her forehead and stopped.

In the faint glow of the moonlight I saw that her lips were moving. The rest of her body remained completely still.

I leaned my ear closer. Her voice was no more than a whisper, but I could understand each word that she was saying as clearly as I could see the clouds in a midday sky.

“Do you hear it? The chiming of the bell. It’s calling. The bell is calling me.”

As the last days of autumn faded into winter, the sun’s strength began to weaken. Despite the confines of the greenhouse, it wasn’t long before Reya’s proud collection of plants and flowers began to wilt. Needless to say, she did not take the change well. Months of love and nurture proved fruitless against the inevitable death that winter sewed.

If it wasn’t for the resilience of the moonbelle flower, I think that Reya would have completely shattered. Many of the plants surrounding the moonbelle had withered, but the moonbelle itself sat upon its throne, a beacon of life. The flower’s colors were still bold, and showed promise that the harsh winter would not be so quick to put it down.

As the days grew shorter, it felt as if my work shifts only stretched longer. I would return home long after sunset to find Reya in the sunroom, desperately trying to breathe life into a fruitless battle. She wept, and her sadness was contagious. There was nothing that could stop the slow and painful decay of the empire that Reya had cultivated.

Fruit flies returned not long into December. Attracted by the scent of rot, they began to infest the sunroom, multiplying day after day. The plague had spread to even the most resilient of Reya’s ferns. Evergreen plants that were supposed to have no trouble surviving the winter outside had lost their pines.

By Christmas, the only color left in the room was the moonbelle. Lustrous and beautiful as ever, the flower did not just survive, but thrived in the company of death. Reya didn’t have the heart to remove the other plants from the sunroom, but she still spent countless hours in there treating the moonbelle with as much love and tender care as she could offer. Over the course of December, the moonbelle had flourished and grown in an exponential way. When Reya returned to bed one night after repotting the moonbelle for the third time, she looked completely drained.

“It’s not your fault.” I said, knowing what she was upset about. Her plants were the only thing that she had bothered to care about recently. “It was a particularly harsh winter. At least you still have the moonbelle. That will have to be enough until we get a fresh start in spring.”

Reya’s eyes did not meet my own when she spoke.

“It needs company. It doesn’t like being alone. It can’t go all winter like this.”

I scoffed and put down my book.

“What do you mean it doesn’t ‘like’ being alone?”

She never answered my question. Instead, she crawled into bed and pulled the covers over her body, turning away.

“Goodnight, Mike.”

I didn’t pick my book back up. Instead, I stared at the pitiful state that my girlfriend was in over the condition of her garden. She hadn’t been this much of a wreck since her Meemi had passed almost five years ago. The isolation of our home and the bitter winter had worn down her psyche. In the morning I was going to suggest that she search for a therapist, or at the very least encourage her to spend more time with ladies on our street.

I flicked off the light and closed my eyes.

I woke shrouded in twilight, enough light to see that Reya's side of the bed was empty. The light in our bathroom was off, but the door to our bedroom hung wide open.

“Reya?” I asked into the darkness in a whisper.

No response.

I stepped out of bed, my bare feet cold against the creaking wooden floor.

“Reya!” I tried again. This time louder.

No response.

My heartrate quickened as I made my way down the stairs. I knew where I would find her. And the thought of it filled me with inexplicable dread.

The old wooden stairs cried warnings as I descended. The house was lit only by moonlight casting its rays through the windows. My shadow danced as I moved slowly and cautiously through the empty kitchen and into the dining room.

I heard the thumping in my chest as I found the sunroom door wide open.

My mind screamed at me to run. To turn back now and return to the safety of my room.

But I couldn’t. Not when Reya needed help.

If I was being honest, she had needed it for a while now, and it was my fault that I had ignored the signs for so long. Her obsession with gardening had manifested itself as a toxic relationship that she could not pry herself from.

So I would have to intervene.

I would go into the sunroom and get rid of every dead plant that she still tried to cling onto. Then I would remove the moonbelle as well. I needed to sever the tie completely so that she could start over and begin to heal. It would be difficult, but she would thank me for it eventually.

The surge of confidence that had swelled within me was just as quickly stolen from me as I rounded the corner and faced the scene in the sunroom.

I had expected Reya to be there, but I never could have prepared myself for what else I was about to confront.

The glass paneled ceiling of the sunroom let in a blanket of moonlight that illuminated Reya’s crouched body as she hunched near the base of the table that displayed the moonbelle. Her back was arched and she was on her knees with both of her forearms pressed to the ground as if she were in prayer. Her feet were bare and she wore nothing but a thin nightgown. The moonbelle glowed above her, an idol of worship overseeing its congregants. All of the plants that had died earlier in the winter were completely withered. A substantial amount of dirt and soil had been spilled on the floor and several pots were broken. In the dim light of the moon, I was able to see that Reya’s hands were caked in soil. The dirt was buried deep beneath her long nails, as if she had been fervently digging at the plant’s roots.

The mess of soil and dead plants was completely devoid of color. All life that they had once possessed seemed to be absorbed by the radiating moonbelle flower, perched on its throne.

The army of fruit flies had grown to a throng. Dozens upon dozens of them landed on Reya’s back and hair. I needed to cover my nose to protect myself from the pungent smell of must and death.

“Reya!” I shouted. “Reya!” My voice was loud, but shaky.

The trance that she was in broke and her body snapped out of its praying position.

She looked at me with wide eyes and screamed a piercing wordless screech that injected the fear of God right into my heart. Her scream turned to a loud cry and eventually settled into a low sob.

I ran to her and hesitantly placed a hand on her back.

“It’s okay. I’m here. You were sleepwalking.”

“Mike?” She asked with a fragile voice.

Her body shook uncontrollably and she was unnaturally cold. I pulled her in to share my warmth and I realized that my body was shaking too.

She buried her head into my shoulder.

“I don’t know what I’m doing here.”

I rocked her steadily as I looked up at the moonbelle flower, its bell-shaped petals swayed ever so slightly and in the silence of the night I heard a faint clicking noise. Just looking at the living, breathing, organism filled me with an indescribable sense of dread that made my skin crawl.

She looked down at her hands, and then around at the mess that she had made.

“I’m sorry. I’m sorry.” Her voice was weak. Barely a whisper.

“It’s alright, I have you now. Let’s get you back to bed.”

Reya only nodded as I helped her to her feet.

I made sure to close the sunroom door behind us, leaving the moonbelle flower and the rest of the dead plants there for another night.

I barely slept a wink that night. Thankfully, when the sun rose it was a Saturday morning and I had no obligation to go into work.

There was a mess that needed cleaning, and I had no intentions of putting it off.

Before I got too far, I felt Reya’s hand wrap around my wrist.

When I turned to face her, she appeared haunted, and bags had formed under her eyes.

“Don’t get rid of it.” She said desperately. I stared back at her, my lips forming a thin line. “Please.”

She looked directly into my soul, and she begged. My heart nearly shattered seeing her so forlorn and pale.

I simply couldn’t refuse.

“Okay. You stay here and rest. I’ll clean up the mess in the sunroom.”

Relieved, Reya loosened her grip and rolled back on her side. Her slender arm disappeared on the other side of the covers and I could have sworn it looked like she lost muscle.

“Thank you.” She said while facing away from me.

The catastrophe in the greenhouse was unfortunately just as disastrous as I remembered it being. Thankfully the morning sun made the room substantially less eerie. Still, the simple presence of the moonbelle was enough to make my skin crawl. In the safety of the morning light, I approached the flower, getting closer than I had ever been before. It was just a stupid flower. No different than a rose or an orchid. I could easily grab Reya’s garden scissors and snip the damned thing right at the stem. It would be easy.

The moonbelle looked back at me, knowing that it was safe.

I turned my back on the flower, cursing to myself. I had already personified the thing more than I should have.

I began to work on cleaning the room. First I swept, then mopped, and finally began to dump out the dead plants in the woods in the backyard, all while the moonbelle watched me from its dais.

I was on my fourth trip to the woods to dispose of another handful of withered plants when I heard Ms. Quinn.

“Morning! This is a rare sight! Seeing you do the gardening for a change.”

I muttered to myself as I turned to face our neighbor who had shown herself into our unfenced backyard.

“I tried the front door several times. No one answered, but I saw both cars in the drive so I figured you’d be back here.”

“Or perhaps we simply aren’t accepting guests at the moment. Good morning to you too, Ms. Quinn.” I said as I met her outside the sunroom.

If Ms. Quinn noticed that all of the other plants in the sunroom were missing, she didn’t comment.

“Please Mike, throw this old lady a bone and call me Lily!”

“Of course. My apologies. What brings you around, Lily?”

The word felt unnatural on my lips.

Ms. Quinn’s demeanor shifted, and for once she didn’t bother to present a jovial facade.

“Well, I’m actually here to check in on Reya. She hasn’t been to the last few ladies' nights and I wanted to make sure she’s doing alright.”

“She’s a bit sick, that’s all. I’ll send her your regards.”

“What a nasty sickness it must be to have lasted so long. Do you mind if I come in and make her some tea?”

I made sure to hold my position in front of the sunroom door.

“I don’t think that’s such a great idea. Might be contagious.”

I considered faking a cough, but it would be painfully obvious.

Ms. Quinn’s eyes flicked up and I followed them. I nearly jumped when I saw Reya staring down at us from the porthole window of our bedroom, her face was as ghastly as ever.

How long had she been watching us?

Ms. Quinn gave a halfhearted wave that Reya returned.

Ms. Quinn’s eyes were hard when they returned to meet mine. She spoke in a low and direct voice.

“Reya is a wonderful girl. I care very much about her wellbeing. You do well to care for her too. Do you hear?”

I clenched my jaw, feeling as if I was being accused of something.

Ms. Quinn looked up to Reya and smiled before she left. As she rounded the house her eyes fell on the sunroom windows and she studied the single moonbelle plant that remained in the almost vacant space.

That night, I dreamt. A dream so real that I could almost feel it. Despite my lucidity, I could not force myself to wake.

My body was soil. I was a bed for which the moonbelle would grow from. The flower - that damned flower sat atop of me and it grew into me. I watched my own body as its roots penetrated the bend of my elbows and began to spread through my veins. I could feel its territory expanding, growing beneath my skin and claiming my body for itself. It grew within me, exploring my body and creating a labyrinth of roots that began to tie themselves around each other. I attempted to scream but no words came out as the moonbelle’s roots snaked upwards beneath my cheeks and worked their way to my cranium. They buried under my skull and wrapped themselves around my brain.

The moonbelle sat above me, a hypnotic shade of purple. My eyes became stuck as I stared at the flower. It had sedated me completely, holding me completely transfixed. The flower swayed, and then began to ring. The sound of a bell grew louder and louder until it became a cacophonous echo of thunder.

I bolted awake with a start. I didn’t have any time to gather myself before I noticed Reya sitting upright in the bed, already awake. In the glow of moonlight from our window I saw something that made my stomach drop.

The moonbelle. Reya had brought it to our room and placed it upon our bed. It had no pot, instead, the flower was planted in what must have been several pounds of gardening soil on top of our comforter.

Reya hummed to herself as she stroked at the flower’s stem with the back of her dirty nails.

“Reya! What the fuck! What did you do!” The moonbelle and its bed of soil sat atop of me and I flung the covers off, sending the flower and a spray of dirt to the other side of the bed.

“No!” Reya let out a piercing scream as she snatched up the plant. She put herself uncomfortably close to the flower’s bell-shaped petals and began to whisper something that I couldn’t hear.

My heart was beating out of my chest and I was paralyzed in shock. Reya turned to face me. She crawled across the bed quickly and placed her hand on my chest. Her eyes were wide, and even in the darkness I could make out how severe the bags beneath them were.

“I’m sorry. We didn’t mean to startle you. The moonbelle didn’t want to be in the greenhouse anymore. It didn’t want to be alone.”

I shook my head. “You need help.” Was all that I could manage to say.

“Michael. Michael. Michael. Michael.” She was so close that I could smell the dirt on her breath as she spoke. In the gloomy light I was able to see a trail of dark brown soil dribbling down her chin and coating her lips.

“How don’t you see it? This is our life. Our future. Don’t you want that? To grow with me and spread our roots as a family.”

I eyed the moonbelle on the other side of the bed and Reya turned my cheek with her hand to face her.

“Do you love me?” Her expression turned impossibly cold.

“I do love you!” I tried to pull myself away, but despite her frail arms she held me in place. “I do! But something is seriously wrong, Reya. We’ll get you the help that you need.”

Reya placed her palm on my cheek and studied my face.

When she spoke, her voice was a whisper.

“If you love me, then put a baby in me. Plant your seed so that it can grow within me.” Her hands wrapped around me, twisting like vines. “Let us cultivate our baby and raise it so that it flourishes with life. We can start our own garden. Our own family.”

With a burst of strength I shoved her off of me.

The moonbelle had completely broken Reya, but I could still fix it. It wasn’t too late to protect her from herself.

I lurched forward, reaching for the moonbelle. I never made it to my target. Reya was impossibly quick to put herself in my way.

I only had a moment to realize what she was holding before she pressed down on the trigger of the pepper spray that I had gotten her. My eyes began to burn with excruciating pain. It felt as if someone had taken hot oil and dropped it directly onto my eyeballs.

“Fuck!” I cried out in agony.

The next thing I knew, Reya used her shoulder to shove the weight of her entire body into my chest and I was sent tumbling. My head rattled and my eyes remained shut as they spasmed. The pain made them impossible to open.

Reya leapt from the bed and dragged me by my collar out of the bedroom with impressive strength.

“Reya stop!” I called as I flailed my arms. For a moment, fear consumed me as I felt the empty space beside me. It was the long flight of wooden stairs. I anticipated that I may soon be sent plummeting down them.

To my relief, Reya dropped my collar before leaping back into the bedroom and slamming the door. I heard the lock click and the movement of our dresser before I was able to regain any semblance of balance.

I wiped violently at my tear-soaked eyes. I pushed desperately for them to open, but the pain was still too great. I fell into a coughing fit, feeling the chemical burn in my lungs.

Using only my hands as guides, I fled down the stairs and tapped my way to the kitchen. Wincing in pain, I stood over the sink, rinsing my eyes until the discomfort dropped to a manageable level.

I patted the pocket of my shorts, grateful to have fallen asleep before plugging in my phone to charge.

Through squinted eyes I could see that my lock screen read 6:03 AM. It was surprisingly early.

I began to dial 9-1-1 when I stopped.

What the hell was I going to say? That my girlfriend was possessed by a flower? And what would they do when they got here? Bust down the door and take her away to the nearest hospital that was two hours away?

She needed immediate help. And there was only one person in this small town that knew anything about the moonbelle.

With trembling fingers, I swiped through my contacts and stopped at Cindy’s number.

It rang several times before going to voicemail.

“Come on! Don’t you florists always get up at the crack of dawn!” I said as I pressed redial.

Still no answer.

This time, I left a message.

“Cindy, it’s Mike. It has Reya. The flower. The fucking moonbelle flower has her. You need to tell me how to fix it. I… It doesn’t make any sense. She’s lost her mind and I don’t know what to do.”

Cursing to myself, I ended the voicemail and grabbed my keys. I ran out of the front door wearing nothing but the clothes that I had fallen asleep in. The crisp winter air bit at my skin, and when I touched my steering wheel it made my fingers numb.

The drive passed in a blur of colors and trees that my brain didn’t bother to register and I arrived at a completely empty parking lot.

I got out of my car and ran to the glass door, banging on it loudly.

“Cindy! Let me in!”

I tried the door and surprisingly I found it unlocked.

I stepped inside and I nearly threw up from the repulsive stench.

From the outside it hadn’t been so obvious that every single plant in the entire store had rotted. All except one. A single indigo bell-shaped flower sat in the center of the small store, unphased by the death that surrounded it.

I approached the moonbelle hesitantly.

The flower’s roots were impossibly long, spreading across the entire floor and up the walls of Green Garden.

As I got closer, I was finally able to identify the source of the foul smell.

Buried in the roots of the moonbelle was what was left of Cindy’s lifeless body. Only recognizable by the apron that she wore around her neck, the level of decomposition should have been impossible. She was a thin wrapping of grey skin around bones and nothing more. Her blonde hair had turned to wisps of white, and her cheekbones were aggressively sharp. Any hint of vitality that the poor woman had once shown had completely decayed into a form so lifeless.

Through the fear and the disgust, I pushed forward. I leapt over Cindy’s body and I went straight for the moonbelle. I plucked it from its soil and I began to shred the plant with my bare hands. It was as easy as I had imagined it to be to pull the bell from its stem. I didn’t stop until every leaf and every petal had been ripped from the flower. For good measure, I threw the remnants to the ground and squished them under my bare heel.

Relief flooded over me as I looked down to the scraps of flower at my feet.

It can die.

I sprinted out of Green Garden and hopped into my car that was still running.

I was out of the parking lot and speeding home before the glass door crashed shut causing the welcome bell to chime to a vacant store.

My mind was a whirlwind as I blew past empty country roads, gunning it home.

Should I call the police? For Cindy, or for Reya? Or for both.

They wouldn’t understand.

There wasn’t time.

The sun had risen past the horizon by the time that I slid my car into the driveway, nearly crashing into Reya’s.

I had no plan. I only knew that I needed to destroy that flower.

The soles of my bare feet slapped against hard wood as I barreled upstairs. The bedroom door was locked and when I shoved against it, I felt the weight of our dresser pushing back against me.

“Reya! You need to open up!”

“You can’t come in.” Reya said from the other side. Her voice a faint whisper. It sounded as if she didn’t have the strength to speak any louder.

“Rey, please listen to me. It’s the flower. It has some sort of hold on you. It’s draining you of your life and it is going to kill you like it killed the rest of your plants. You need to destroy it.”

“I… can’t. I won’t. I gave it life. It must survive. It must grow. That is why you need to stay out there.”

Her words were unconfident, like deep down she knew that they were not her own.

Was I speaking to her, or was I speaking to the moonbelle?

“I was wrong. I’m sorry that I didn’t understand before, but I do now. The moonbelle. It’s beautiful. When I look at it I see my life with you. I want to be with you and grow together. We can give life to children as you gave to the flower.” The lies flooded out of me, desperately searching for anything I could say that would convince Reya to let me in.

“The bells. Mike, do you hear them? Do you listen to their chimes?”

“Yes.” I lied. “I can hear them.”

“They’re so loud.” Reya’s voice grew even more faint. “I don’t think that I want to hear them anymore.”

“Reya, stay with me! Open the door and I can make it all go away!”

For a moment, I thought I heard the dresser beginning to slide. The sound was cut short by the sound of a body dropping to the floor on the other side of the door.

“Reya, no!”

I slammed my shoulder against the door while trying the knob. I stepped back and I kicked. I threw myself at the door again and again, until the frame began to splinter. With all my strength, I pushed against it, opening it just a crack. I could see the edge of the dresser that blocked my path. It was too heavy. I would never be able to bust through.

“Shit!”  I exclaimed.

Short of boring a hole in the wall, there was only one other way into our bedroom. The porthole window.

I ran down the stairs, my body fighting exhaustion. I entered the backyard and looked up at the window to our bedroom. It was open, revealing a sprawling set of thick roots that belonged to the moonbelle.

I started to climb. Using a step ladder, I made my way onto the roof of the sunroom. My feet felt numb standing on the thick glass roofing. The roots were out of my reach meaning that I would need to jump. Even if I grabbed hold, I had no way of knowing if they would be able to support my weight.

I didn’t have time to think or hesitate. Reya was up there and she needed me.

I steeled my nerves. And then I leapt.

My body crashed against the house as I clutched onto one of the dangling roots. My second hand matched the hold before the moonbelle began to react. The roots flailed like tentacles whipping me from side to side.

I heard it then. The bells. Thundering chimes, almost like clicking, echoed out of the bedroom window. It was a cry for help. The moonbelle knew that it was in danger, and that gave me the strength I needed to push.

I climbed, scrappy and unsteady. Every thrash, kick, and pull brought me inches closer to the porthole. Roots had wrapped around my thigh, attempting to drag me down, while others attacked my face, forcing themselves into my eyes and mouth.

I let out a warcry as I finally managed to grip the window and with both hands I pulled myself inside. My body crashed to the ground in the nest of the beast. The moonbelle was atop our bed, swaying violently side to side as it commanded its earthy tendrils to keep me at bay. My muscles ached as I pushed forward, snapping and ripping my way through the woven defences.

I  reached the bed and with one hand I grabbed the moonbelle by its stem and brought it up to eye level.  I snapped the petals from the stem, as if I was breaking its neck. As soon as I did, the flailing roots around me dropped to the ground.

Using my bare and bloodied hands, I continued to shred the flower. Fueled by rage and pain, I didn’t stop until the plant was an unrecognizable heap of indigo and green.

Spotting Reya’s collapsed body at the base of the dresser, I dropped what remained of the moonbelle and crawled over to her.

“Reya! Can you hear me?”

I turned her over and checked her pulse. I began to sob as I felt the faint beat of her heart. Her heart that was large enough to find love for a miserable man like me. Her heart that had a way of effortlessly connecting with everyone around her. Her heart that gave her a glow that I wanted to live in for the rest of our lives.

She was pale and on the brink of death, but her eyes fluttered open weakly. My crying turned into a full stream of tears.

“Mike.” She said quietly. She coughed several times before she gathered herself.

“I’m here. I have you.”

Reya looked around the room, taking in the carnage and destruction. Soil and roots covered the walls and floor. Her eyes stopped when they reached what remained of the moonbelle flower on our bed.

She curled closer to me.

“I think I need a new hobby.”

We moved back to New York less than two weeks later. Our little house outside of the city was much smaller than the one on Lotus Lane. I had to take a pay cut to move back, but it was worth every penny.

It took some time, but eventually life returned to normal.

On the seventeenth of July on a Hudson River dinner cruise I got down on one knee and I asked Reya to be my wife. She, thankfully, said yes and we enjoyed the rest of the night in luxury far, far away from the nearest garden. Our only concern was what we were going to do regarding a florist for the wedding day.

Just when I thought I had no more space for joy in my life, we learned that we were also expecting our first baby. We’ve been discussing baby names for a while, but I’ve secretly had my fingers crossed for a girl. For some reason I have always liked the name Quinn. I could see it in Reya’s glow that she finally had everything that she had ever wanted. I already knew that she would make for an incredible mother.

Despite the soon to be arrival of our newest family member, and the monumental task of planning a wedding, Reya decided that she wanted to return to her job at the middle school. Thanks to school hours she usually returned home earlier than me, but today she had a doctor’s appointment scheduled so I had the duty of doing the basic after-work chores.

I was shifting through the usual spam found in our mail when a handwritten envelope stood out to me. The return address was listed as Lotus Lane and I smiled as I thought about Ms. Quinn, who could just have easily sent a text. Reya had a friend for life with that one.

I opened it and withdrew the letter.

“Reya,

The ladies here miss you dearly, but we are so happy to hear about your daughter and the upcoming wedding! It will be such a wonderful night.”

I rolled my eyes, smiling at her *wonderful* vocabulary.

“I’m sorry that the sale of your old house has taken so long. But after the reconstruction it did finally manage to get off the market. I promise that whoever moves into that home will not hold a candle to you, darling! The estate sale happened last week and I stopped by to see what goodies you left for me! I have all the furniture an old lady could ask for, but I did manage to grab those lovely mugs that I always adored.”

When we abandoned Lotus Lane we did not leave the house in pristine condition. It had been a headache getting cleaners and realtors to handle the property from so far away, so at a certain point Reya and I agreed to sell everything that we left behind.

“Oh, and one more thing! There were seeds in the sunroom. They weren’t labeled, but I hope to one day grow a flower as beautiful as you, my dear. I decided to share my haul with the other Lotus Lane ladies, that way we always have something to remember you by. I have included a few of the seeds with this letter as well so that you will have a piece of Lotus Lane with you wherever you go.

With Love, Lily Quinn”

My hand trembled as I turned the envelope over. A small packet of white seeds fell onto the counter. I picked it up and examined one as my heart sank to my stomach. Very, very distantly I heard church bells begin to ring.

I reached for my phone, unable to grab it before the front door flew open.

It was Reya and she ran towards me, her face twisted in a horrified expression. I didn’t have time to explain the note before Reya started rambling. Her voice was shaking with uncontrollable fear and it looked as if she was about to burst into tears.

“They did the ultrasound today and something wasn’t right. The doctor let me put the stethoscope to my ears, but when I did, I didn’t hear a baby’s heartbeat.”

Reya’s voice shattered.

“I heard bells.”
