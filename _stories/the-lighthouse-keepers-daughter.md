Every night for eleven years, Mara climbed the hundred and four steps and lit the lamp for ships that never came.

Her father had kept the light before her, and his mother before him. The shipping lanes had moved north a generation ago, but nobody had told the lighthouse, and nobody had told Mara, and so the beam went on sweeping its slow circle over an empty sea.

She didn't mind. The light was not really for ships. It was a way of saying *someone is here* to whatever was out there in the dark.

***

The ship came on a Tuesday in November, low in the water, one mast snapped clean. Mara watched it through the brass telescope for an hour before she believed it.

By morning there were seven strangers in her kitchen, wrapped in her father's old wool blankets, drinking her coffee, asking her the name of this place.

"It doesn't have one," she said. "It's just the light."

The captain, a woman with salt in the creases of her face, looked at her for a long moment. "We saw it from forty miles out," she said. "We thought we were dreaming."

Mara poured more coffee. Outside, the lamp was dark for the day, resting, the way it always did. Waiting for evening, when it would say again what it had always said.

*Someone is here.*
